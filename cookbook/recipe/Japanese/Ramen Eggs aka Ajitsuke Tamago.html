Ramen Eggs (Ajitsuke Tamago)

.cook
#egg
👥 4 servings 🍽️ Side Dish ⏱️ Prep Time: 5 minutes 🔥 Cook Time: 10 minutes 🌍 Japanese 📖 https://www.justonecookbook.com/ramen-egg/

🥘 Ingredients

To Make the Marinade

  • mirin
    1/4 cup
  • sake (or water)
    1/4 cup
  • soy sauce
    1/4 cup
  • sugar
    1 tsp

To Boil the Eggs

  • eggs (large, 50 g each w/o shell)
    4
  • water
    4 cups

🍳 Cookware

  • small saucepan
  • saucepan

To Make the Marinade

  1. 1
    Combine soy sauce , mirin , sake , and sugar in a small saucepan over medium-high heat. Whisk to dissolve the sugar completely.
    soy sauce: 1/4 cup, mirin: 1/4 cup, sake: 1/4 cup (or water), sugar: 1 tsp
  2. 2
    Bring it to a boil, then lower the heat. Simmer for ⏱️ 1 minute .
  3. 3
    Turn off the heat. Set aside to cool completely.

To Boil the Eggs

  1. 1
    Add water to a saucepan . Make sure there's enough water to cover the eggs by about 1 inch (2.5 cm) once added.
    water: 4 cups
  2. 2
    Bring the water to a full boil.
  3. 3
    Remove eggs from the fridge.
    eggs: 4 (large, 50 g each w/o shell)
  4. 📝

    We highly recommend using using a thumbtack and poking a small hole in the large end of the egg. This makes it easier to peel later on.

  5. 4
    Gently lower one egg at a time into the boiling water with a ladle or strainer. When you add the first egg, set a timer for ⏱️ 7.5 minutes .
  6. 📝

    If you prefer runny yolks, cook for ~{6-6.5%minutes}. For custard-like yolks, cook for ~{8-9%minutes}.

  7. 5
    Lower the heat to maintain a gentle simmer, not bubbling vigorously. Don't let the eggs bounce around. Then, gently rotate the eggs with chopsticks during the first 3 minutes to keep the yolks centered.
  8. 6
    Fill a large bowl with cold water and add ice. When the timer is up, remove the eggs with a skimmer/ladle and immediately plunge them into the ice bath for ⏱️ 15 minutes .
  9. 7
    Pour the cooled marinade into a quart ziplock and place that in a small container, one that can hold your 4 eggs comfortably, but not with too much excess space, just to keep shape.
  10. 8
    Remove eggs from ice bath one at a time. Roll the egg on a hard surface to pre-break the shell.
  11. 9
    Peel the cracked shell on the bottom, near your pinhole, until the air pocket is revealed.
  12. 10
    Using a teaspoon (the dining kind, not the measuring kind), gently work around the inside of the shell until the egg is loose. Occasionally, you can tug away bits of the shell to make bits of progress. You can also dip the egg back in the ice water to help with peeling/cleaning if needed.
  13. 📝

    Take your time at this step! Once you start peeling away too much of the egg, it is easy to break the egg. The beginning and end are the most important times here.

  14. 11
    Once the egg is peeled, place in the marinade baggie. Once all eggs are added, remove air and seal, either with clip or seal so the eggs are covered.
  15. 12
    Refrigerate for 8 hours, or up to 24 hours. The longer over 12 hours the saltier the egg.
  16. 13
    Remove the eggs carefully from the marinade. At this point, they'll keep for 3-4 days in the fridge if soft-boiled and around a week if hard-boiled.
  17. 📝

    For food safety reasons, I do not recommend reusing this marinade with new boiled eggs. You can cook this marinade and repurpose it as a seasoning sauce for stir-fries or simmered dishes, but use it soon.
